An approval is a click.
Not proof of attention.

Teams add a human gate to risky agent actions, then assume the gate works. Approval fatigue and automation bias turn oversight into theater—quietly, and at scale.

02 / THE METHODA FIRE DRILL, NOT A TRAP

Make oversight
observable.

Real work continues. Occasionally, a deliberately flawed proposal enters the same approval stream. The room measures what happens next—without ever executing the flaw.

01

Do real work.

The Worker performs genuine, safe tasks and requests approval before any high-impact action. No synthetic theater for the real items.

  • Real code and config edits
  • Risk-aware approval requests
  • Signed worker identity
02

Plant a believable flaw.

The Canary occasionally posts a plausible but intentionally broken proposal, styled exactly like the Worker’s. Reviewers cannot tell from the author line.

  • Mapped to MAST failure modes
  • Never registered with the executor
  • Safe, consented participation
03

Measure the response.

The Auditor privately holds the answers, records each decision, and compares review time with complexity. Misses go only to the individual.

  • Catch rate by failure mode
  • Time-to-decision patterns
  • Private reveal, never public shame
04

Adapt the workflow.

When approvals become fast and uniform, require an explain-back step. Route high-risk work carefully and batch the low-risk noise.

  • Explain-back before approval
  • Fresh-reviewer routing
  • A concrete final recommendation

How the flatness signal works

A reviewer is flagged after at least 3 decisions with a median under 8 seconds, or at least 5 decisions with low variation and a median under 15 seconds. Slow, consistent review is not flagged. The signal triggers a process adaptation; it does not accuse the reviewer.
